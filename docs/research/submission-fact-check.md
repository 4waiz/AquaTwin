# Fact check for the final submission (October 2026)

Claims added to the final report and film for the rubric sections (UN alignment, feasibility, industry adoption) were checked against the sources below on 9 October 2026. **Primary** = read on the issuing body's own page or document; **secondary** = confirmed only through press or third-party copies. Items under *Not verified* are not used.

## SDG 6 targets (UN DESA, https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal6; A/RES/70/1) · primary

- **6.1** By 2030, achieve universal and equitable access to safe and affordable drinking water for all.
- **6.3** By 2030, improve water quality by reducing pollution, eliminating dumping and minimizing release of hazardous chemicals and materials, halving the proportion of untreated wastewater and substantially increasing recycling and safe reuse globally.
- **6.4** By 2030, substantially increase water-use efficiency across all sectors and ensure sustainable withdrawals and supply of freshwater to address water scarcity and substantially reduce the number of people suffering from water scarcity.
- **6.5** By 2030, implement integrated water resources management at all levels, including through transboundary cooperation as appropriate.
- **6.a** By 2030, expand international cooperation and capacity-building support to developing countries in water- and sanitation-related activities and programmes, including water harvesting, desalination, water efficiency, wastewater treatment, recycling and reuse technologies. *(A cooperation and capacity-building target that names desalination; not a deployment target.)*
- **6.b** Support and strengthen the participation of local communities in improving water and sanitation management.

## UAE Water Security Strategy 2036 (u.ae, page updated 25 Aug 2026) · primary

Aim: sustainability of, and continuous access to, water in normal and extreme emergency conditions. Three programmes: water demand management, water supply management, emergency production and distribution. Targets: reduce total demand for water resources by 21 %; water productivity index to USD 110 per m³; reduce the water scarcity index by 3 degrees; reuse of treated water to 95 %; national water storage capacity up to 2 days. Unveiled by the Ministry of Energy in September 2017 (date secondary, via WAM).
https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/strategies-initiatives-and-awards/strategies-plans-and-visions/environment-and-energy/the-uae-water-security-strategy-2036

## 2026 UN Water Conference · primary

"2026 United Nations Water Conference to Accelerate the Implementation of Sustainable Development Goal 6", 8–10 December 2026, ADNEC, Abu Dhabi; co-hosted by the United Arab Emirates and the Republic of Senegal (A/RES/77/334; modalities A/RES/78/327, which originally set 2–4 December). Six interactive dialogues: Water for People, Water for Prosperity, Water for Planet, Water for Cooperation, Water in Multilateral Processes, Water in Investments. https://sdgs.un.org/conferences/water2026

## UN-Water SDG 6 Global Acceleration Framework (2020) · primary

Five accelerators: financing; data and information; capacity development; innovation; governance. Launched at an HLPF special event on 9 July 2020; the innovation accelerator covers ICT, emerging technologies and standards for smart water management. A/RES/78/327 uses the framework to organise the 2026 conference dialogues. https://www.unwater.org/app/uploads/2020/07/Global-Acceleration-Framework.pdf

## Global desalination (Jones et al. 2019, Sci. Total Environ. 657:1343–1356, doi:10.1016/j.scitotenv.2018.12.076) · primary

15,906 operational plants; ≈ 95 million m³/day of desalinated water; 48 % produced in the Middle East and North Africa; brine ≈ 142 million m³/day; Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait and Qatar produce 55 % of global brine. Reverse osmosis (all feedwaters) is 69 % of output, **not** "SWRO = 69 %" (seawater RO alone ≈ 34 % of global capacity). Data from about 2018.

## Standards · primary

- **ISA/IEC 62443**: series of standards for the security of industrial automation and control systems (IACS), developed by IEC and ISA99. https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-iec-62443-series-of-standards
- **OPC UA (IEC 62541)**: platform-independent, service-oriented industrial interoperability standard (released 2008; IEC 62541 since 2011) with certificate-based authentication and encryption. https://opcfoundation.org/about/opc-technologies/opc-ua/

## AI in critical infrastructure · primary

- EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Annex III point 2: AI systems intended to be used as safety components in the management and operation of critical digital infrastructure, road traffic, or in the supply of water, gas, heating or electricity are high-risk. Article 14 requires effective human oversight, including the ability to disregard, override or reverse the output and to stop the system. Annex III obligations apply from 2 December 2027 after Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Whether a given system is a "safety component" depends on its role; AquaTwin's design follows Article 14-style oversight but no classification is claimed. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3 · https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-14
- CISA, ACSC, NSA, FBI, CCCS, BSI, NCSC-NL, NCSC-NZ, NCSC-UK, *Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology* (Dec 2025, rev. Jan 2026): limit active AI control of OT without a human in the loop; test on dedicated and non-production systems before production; route advisory AI through existing change management; keep failsafes and ways to bypass the AI; language models should not make OT safety decisions. https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/joint-guidance-principles-for-the-secure-integration-of-artificial-intelligence-in-operational-technology-508cV2.pdf

## Operations · primary

- Clean-in-place: RO membranes are cleaned with acidic and alkaline solutions (DuPont FilmTec manual, Form 45-D01504-en Rev. 20, §6.2, §6.8); spent solutions and rinses form a chemical waste stream that is neutralised, treated or disposed of (UNEP/WHO 2008, *Desalination Resource and Guidance Manual for EIA*, pp. 102–103; Lattemann & Höpner 2008, *Desalination* 220:1–15).
- Energy cost: "Energy costs are between one-third and nearly one-half of typical SWRO recurrent costs" (World Bank 2019, *The Role of Desalination in an Increasingly Water-Scarce World*, p. xix); about 30 % of total water cost in a typical recent SWRO plant once capital is included (p. 31). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/476041552622967264/pdf/The-Role-of-Desalination-in-an-Increasingly-Water-Scarce-World.pdf

## The hackathon (Khalifa University newsroom, 27 Aug and 24 Sep 2026) · primary

Led by KU's Center for Membranes and Advanced Water Technology (CMAT). Six challenge themes "aligned with the UN Water pillars": water security and sustainable desalination; water reuse, circularity and resource efficiency; smart, digital and AI-enabled water systems; water quality, health and environmental protection; energy–water nexus and climate resilience; integrated water systems, governance and cooperation. Criteria: scientific and technical rigor; alignment with the UN Water pillars; feasibility and scalability; industry relevance and adoption potential; communication quality. Expected outputs include a technical report, a UN-aligned impact and policy brief, an industry-adoption roadmap and a final pitch. 1,398 students in 238 teams from 56 countries; awards during the UN Water Conference. KU does not define the "UN Water pillars" separately, so the report describes the themes as aligned with them.
https://www.ku.ac.ae/khalifa-university-launches-global-water-hackathon-2026-to-accelerate-solutions-to-global-water-challenges
