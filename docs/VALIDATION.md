# Validation

> Generated by `scripts/report/validation_md.py` from `public/data/validation/results.json` (created 2026-09-29 20:30:18 UTC, 5 seeds, 270 s) and `public/data/models/ml-metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand — re-run the script after the experiments.

**What this is:** controlled experiments on the simulated reference plant (docs/MODEL.md §2), where the true state is known. AquaTwin sees only noisy telemetry. **What this is not:** validation on an industrial plant. All results below are simulation results.

## 1. Questions

1. Does the hybrid (physics + ML residual) predict the plant better than physics alone or ML alone — inside and outside the conditions it was trained on?
2. Are its uncertainty estimates honest, and does it know when it is outside its envelope?
3. In closed loop (hourly re-optimisation screened by AquaGuard), does better prediction translate into fewer constraint violations, and at what energy cost?
4. Does model-based monitoring warn earlier than conventional threshold alarms?

**Out-of-distribution withhold rule** (confidence < 50 %): flags 77 % of out-of-envelope samples and 0.01 % of in-envelope samples (by excursion type: salinity 100 %, temperature 99 %, both 100 %, fouling only 7 %).

**Reading:** inside the envelope the hybrid is the most accurate on every target. Outside it, the ML-only model degrades most; the hybrid degrades less because its physics backbone still extrapolates in the right direction, but its intervals under-cover — the reason AquaTwin withholds recommendations there rather than trusting them. Heavier fouling alone is poorly detected from inputs (it changes the plant, not the operating conditions); that is left to the health monitoring.

## 4. Early-warning lead time (no-action runs)

For each scenario the no-action run is monitored by each model. The critical event is the first unscheduled constraint violation, cleaning-threshold crossing or capacity loss on the true plant. Only warnings related to that event count (e.g. a quality warning before a TDS violation). The energy-cap scenario is excluded: curtailment is announced in advance, so lead time is not a model property.

| Scenario | First critical event | Conventional alarm | Physics | ML only | **Hybrid** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salinity shock | permeate quality at +2.7 h | 20 min | 1.0 h | 1.0 h | **50 min** |
| Membrane fouling | flow cleaning criterion (−10 %), Train 2 at +18.2 h | no warning | 11.6 h | 12.6 h | **12.5 h** |
| Demand surge | storage minimum at +8.7 h | 1.3 h | 6.8 h | 6.7 h | **6.8 h** |
| Compound extreme | permeate quality at +1.8 h | 10 min | 20 min | 20 min | **20 min** |

Note on the membrane-fouling row: Train 2 starts with a normalised pressure drop about 20 % above its clean baseline, which already meets the manufacturer's pressure-drop cleaning criterion (+10–15 %) at t = 0; AquaTwin reports that immediately. The lead time concerns the flow criterion (−10 %), which alarms on absolute values miss.

Conventional alarms: permeate TDS > 95 % of limit, storage < minimum + 5 %, permeate flow −10 % at design pressure, vessel ΔP > 90 % of limit, motor limit. AquaTwin warnings: fouling-trend forecast, 2-hour quality outlook (feed trend extrapolated through the model), storage-depletion forecast. False warnings in normal operation (hours flagged in 24 h): conventional 0.0, physics 0.0, ML only 0.0, hybrid 0.0.

## 5. Findings (computed from the tables above)

- **Constraint violations avoided:** Salinity shock: 21.3 h with fixed operation → 0.0 h with AquaTwin; Energy constraint: 6.0 h with fixed operation → 0.0 h with AquaTwin; Demand surge: 15.3 h with fixed operation → 0.0 h with AquaTwin.
- **Start time:** of 18 start-time runs (Salinity shock, Energy constraint, Demand surge × six start times, seed 1), runs with a violation: Fixed operation 18, Physics only 6, ML only 8, AquaTwin hybrid 0 (§3.8).
- **Early warning:** Salinity shock: hybrid 50 min ahead vs conventional alarm 20 min ahead; Membrane fouling: hybrid 12.5 h ahead vs conventional alarm no warning; Demand surge: hybrid 6.8 h ahead vs conventional alarm 1.3 h ahead; Compound extreme: hybrid 20 min ahead vs conventional alarm 10 min ahead. Model-based warnings help most for slow processes (fouling, storage); for fast ramps the gain is minutes.
- **Energy:** relative to fixed operation, the hybrid loop changes specific energy by 0.0 % to +2.9 % across scenarios (largest: Demand surge). Avoiding violations usually costs energy; lower energy is not claimed.
- **Outside the envelope (compound extreme):** every method violates constraints for 21.9–22.2 h of 24. AquaTwin withholds its recommendation at 19 of 24 decisions, as designed — this limits harm but does not solve the problem.
- **In-loop prediction error:** the hybrid has the lowest production MAE in 8 of 9 in-envelope scenarios (exceptions: Demand surge — physics 6.4, ML only 66.8, hybrid 24.2 m³/h). Each loop visits different operating points, so this is less controlled than the held-out test in §2.
- **Sensor drift:** all models inherit the biased permeate-conductivity signal (in-loop TDS error: physics 16.8, ML only 16.1, hybrid 16.6 mg/L); sensor validation is outside this prototype.

